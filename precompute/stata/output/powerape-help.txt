                                                                    Statistics/Data analysis      
      
      Title
      
          powerape -- Power analysis for average partial effects (APE) and average interaction
                        effects (AIE)
      
      
      Syntax
      
          Describe the world (a data-generating process, DGP) and store it under a name:
      
              powerape dgp name, focal(varspec) baseline(#) [moderator(varspec)
                       covariates(varspecs) correlation(#|matname) signal(#) gamma(numlist)
                       model(probit|logit) nint(#) seedint(#) replace nodisplay]
      
              powerape dgp name [if] [in], empirical focal(varname|varspec) baseline(#)
                       [covariates(varlist) signal(#) gamma(numlist) model(probit|logit) ...]
      
              powerape dgp name, fromfit focal(varname) [baseline(#) ...]        (after probit
                       or logit)
      
              powerape dgp name, panel focal(varspec) nperiods(numlist) baseline(#)
                       [pperiods(numlist) retention(#) rho(#) creshare(#) moderator(varspec)
                       covariates(varspecs) correlation() signal(#) ...]
      
              powerape dgp name, iv focal(varspec) instruments(varspecs) endogeneity(#)
                       ivstrength(#) baseline(#) [moderator(varspec) covariates(varspecs)
                       correlation() signal(#) ...]
      
              powerape dgp name, did baseline(# [#]) [gap(# [#]) trend(# [#]) ptreated(#)
                       design(panel|cross-sections) rho(#) ppost(#) trends(index|linear)
                       analysis(index|linear) npre(#) npost(#) subgroup(varspec)
                       covariates(varspecs) correlation() signal(#) gamma(numlist)
                       model(probit|logit) ...]
      
              powerape dgp name [if] [in], didpilot outcome(varname) group(varname) trend(#)
                       [id(varname) period(varname) options of did]
      
          Pin the effect in effect-size units (the inversion step):
      
              powerape setape name, target(#)
      
              powerape setaie name, target(#) mainfocal(#) mainmoderator(#)
      
              powerape setatt name, target(# [#])        (difference-in-differences designs)
      
          Power, curves, required sample size, minimum detectable effect, robustness:
      
              powerape power name, n(#) [sim_options]
      
              powerape curve name, n(numlist) [sim_options]
      
              powerape n name [, power(#) nrange(# #) maxiter(#) noconfirm nsimconfirm(#)
                       sim_options]
      
              powerape mde name, n(#) [power(#) direction(positive|negative) mainfocal(#)
                       mainmoderator(#) maxiter(#) noconfirm nsimconfirm(#) sim_options]
      
              powerape robust name, n(#) vary(input(values) ...) [pin(ape|coefficients)
                       mode(power|mde) power(#) gridpoints(#) nonmax nmaxpower(#) direction()
                       sim_options]
      
          Reporting and justified error rates:
      
              powerape statement [resname]
      
              powerape alpha [resname] [, cost(#) prior(#) error(minimize|balance) cap(#) size
                       nsimsize(#) seed(#) name(resname)]
      
              powerape powerat [resname], alpha(numlist)
      
              powerape frontier [, cost(#) power(#) prior(#) claim(detect|minimum)]
      
          Housekeeping:
      
              powerape describe name | trueape name | trueaie name | trueatt name | list | drop
                       name|_all | engine [, engine(stata|mata) effects(closed|margins)] |
                       version
      
          sim_options                 Description
          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
            claim(minimum|detect|equivalence)
                                        claim to power; default minimum (detect for mde)
            sesoi(#)                  smallest effect size of interest, in APE units
            alpha(#)                  the claim's error rate; default 0.05
            alpharule(exp)            sample-size rule: a Stata expression in @n, e.g.
                                        alpharule(0.05*sqrt(1000/@n))
            conf(#)                   interval level override for the claim
            nsim(#)                   replications (power 1000, curve 1000, n 1500, mde 1000,
                                        robust 800)
            seed(#)                   seed; the ambient RNG state is restored afterwards
            se(model|robust)          model-based or heteroskedasticity-robust SEs
                                        (cross-sectional routes; ignored where the design fixes
                                        the SEs: panels, IV, and the linear analysis of a
                                        difference-in-differences design)
            separation(fail|keep)     scoring of replications with a separated identifying cell
            engine(stata|mata)        who fits the simulated studies (see Engines) for this
                                        command only; default stata, or the session default set
                                        by powerape engine
            effects(closed|margins)   closed-form delta-method effects or margins/ginteff per
                                        replication, for this command only; default closed, or
                                        the session default
            name(resname)             store the result under resname (default _last)
            nodraws                   do not keep the per-replication draws (power only)
            nodisplay                 suppress output
          --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      
      
      Description
      
          powerape is a Stata port of the R package powerape (version 1.12.0):  simulation-based
          power analysis in which the effect size of interest is the average partial effect
          (APE) or the average interaction effect (AIE) of a probit or logit model. Targets are
          stated in probability points; the package calibrates the data-generating process
          (baseline rate plus nuisance signal), inverts the target into coefficients, checks
          feasibility, and computes power for three confidence-interval claims (Riesthuis,
          2024): detect (the interval excludes zero in the hypothesized direction), minimum (the
          lower bound exceeds the SESOI) and equivalence (the interval lies within +/- SESOI).
          It provides power curves, required sample sizes with a high-precision confirmation
          stage, minimum detectable effects, robustness sweeps with the insurance-premium n_max
          (Hancock and Feng, 2025), citable power statements, and justified error rates (a
          sample-size rule, or the error-cost optimum of Maier and Lakens, 2022).
      
          Routes: parametric covariates with Gaussian-copula dependence; covariates resampled
          from pilot data in memory (empirical); a fitted probit or logit pilot model (fromfit);
          correlated random effects panel probit with Mundlak means, unit-clustered SEs and
          unbalanced panels (panel); and an endogenous focal variable estimated by
          control-function probit, the estimator of Stata's cfprobit (iv). A sixth route, did,
          plans a difference-in-differences study with a binary outcome: its target is the
          effect on the treated in probability points (see Difference-in-differences designs).
      
          Every simulated study is, by default, fit by Stata's own estimation commands (probit,
          logit, cfprobit; see Engines).
      
      
      Variable specifications
      
          A varspec is name, [binary p(#) | normal mean(#) sd(#)] [icc(#)].  The type is
          inferred when omitted: p() means binary, otherwise normal (default mean 0, sd 1).
          icc() is the within-unit persistence on the latent scale and matters only for panel
          DGPs (icc(1) = time-constant). Several variables are given as parenthesized specs:
          covariates((age, normal mean(45) sd(12)) (female, binary p(0.55))).
      
      
      Subcommands
      
          dgp describes the world. baseline() is the average P(Y=1) with the focal (and
              moderator) at their reference values (0 for binary, the mean for continuous);
              signal() is the nuisance covariates' share of the latent-index variance at unit
              error variance (the McKelvey-Zavoina R-squared under probit; a weaker world under
              logit, see the R documentation of ape_dgp); gamma() gives explicit nuisance
              coefficients instead; correlation() is a scalar exchangeable Gaussian-copula
              correlation or a matrix over (focal, moderator, covariates). The panel route is
              probit-only; nperiods() is a scalar (balanced) or a vector of lengths with
              pperiods() probabilities, or a scalar with retention() for monotone attrition;
              rho() is the latent unit-effect share and creshare() the fraction loaded on the
              Mundlak means. The IV route takes endogeneity() (the error correlation) and
              ivstrength() (the instruments' share of the focal's (latent) variance); a binary
              focal gets a probit first stage, a continuous one a linear first stage.
      
          setape / setaie solve the focal (and interaction) coefficients so the true APE / AIE
              equals target(); setaie anchors the two main effects by their
              conditional-at-reference APEs.  Infeasible targets are refused with the attainable
              range.
      
          setatt pins the effect on the treated of a difference-in-differences design (dgp ...,
              did): target() is the treated group's rate after treatment minus the rate it would
              have had without, in probability points. A design with subgroup() takes two
              numbers, the effect in the reference subgroup and then in the other one; its
              estimand is their difference. trueatt recomputes the effect(s) from the stored
              design.
      
          power simulates power at n() and prints the outcome distribution (minimum /
              detect-only / inconclusive / equivalence / failed).  curve does so over a grid. n
              searches the required sample size for power() (default 0.90) and confirms it at
              nsimconfirm() (default 4 x nsim), never trimming n on overshoot. mde searches the
              smallest effect that reaches power() (default 0.80) at a fixed n (for equivalence:
              the tightest margin at the pinned truth). robust re-runs the analysis over a
              factorial grid of contextual inputs (baseline, signal, correlation; rho,
              retention; endogeneity, ivstrength) and reports the worst scenario and n_max.
              Difference-in-differences designs can vary baseline, gap and trend (not with a
              subgroup), rho (panels), ptreated, signal, and the scale on which untreated trends
              are parallel, vary(trends(index linear)). For panel DGPs n() counts units. Sweeps
              over empirical and fromfit DGPs recalibrate from the pilot rows stored in the DGP
              (the data need not be in memory); parametric, panel and IV DGPs are rebuilt from
              their options. The engine subcommands hold and restore the user's e() around the
              simulated fits.
      
          statement prints a citable methods paragraph for the last (or named) power, n, mde or
              alpha result.  alpha chooses the error rate on the stored draws of a power result
              (weighted combined error rate; cap 0.05); powerat re-thresholds those draws at
              other error rates; frontier is the closed-form level at the detectability
              frontier.
      
          version prints the version of the ado-file and of the Mata library lpowerape.mlib with
              their locations and warns when they differ (a stale library after an update); it
              stores r(version), r(ado_version), r(mlib_version) and r(match). engine without
              options shows the session defaults; with options it sets them.  engine() or
              effects() given on a simulation command apply to that command only and leave the
              session defaults unchanged.
      
      
      Difference-in-differences designs
      
          powerape dgp name, did describes a difference-in-differences study with one treatment
          date: a treated and a control group observed before and after the treated group
          receives the intervention, in one period each by default or in npre() periods before
          and npost() periods after. The target is the average treatment effect on the treated
          (ATT) after treatment, in probability points, pinned with powerape setatt. The outcome
          is regressed on the group indicator, period indicators, the treatment indicator
          (treated group after the treatment date) and any covariates.
      
          The world is described on the probability scale by three rates:  baseline() (control
          group, before), gap() (treated minus control, before; default 0) and trend() (the
          control group's change from before to after; default 0). With several periods the
          rates are the same in every period before and in every period after the treatment
          date, and so is the effect. What the treated group's rate would have been after the
          treatment date without treatment depends on the scale on which the two groups'
          untreated trends are parallel, set by trends(): index (default) means parallel on the
          probit/logit index, the assumption under which the probit/logit analysis identifies
          the ATT (Wooldridge, 2023; Puhani, 2012); linear means parallel in probabilities, the
          assumption of the linear analysis (with covariates: parallel on average over each
          group's covariates).  ptreated() is the treated share (default 0.5). Covariates are
          constant over time; correlation() runs over (group, covariates), or over (group,
          subgroup, covariates).
      
          The analysis each simulated study receives is set by analysis():  index (default) is a
          pooled probit or logit, with the ATT as the average partial effect of the treatment
          indicator over the treated post-period observations and a delta-method standard error
          (in Stata:  margins, dydx(w) subpop(if w == 1)); linear is a linear probability model
          fitted by ordinary least squares, with the ATT as the coefficient of the treatment
          indicator (the standard difference-in-differences estimate). Each analysis is unbiased
          only in the world whose scale it assumes.  When trends() and analysis() name the same
          scale, powerape power reports power. When they differ, the analysis centres on another
          number than the ATT: power and curve then report a rejection rate together with the
          mean of the estimates, and n, mde, powerat, alpha and statement refuse the design or
          the result. With two periods and no covariates the data cannot tell the two scales
          apart, and the same four rates imply different effects under each (control .20 to .30
          and treated .40 to .60 is an effect of .100 under linear trends, .074 on the probit
          index and .067 on the logit index): the printed design shows both readings. The linear
          analysis always centres on the cross difference of the four rates.
      
          Data structure. design(panel) (default) follows the same units over all periods (a
          balanced panel); n() counts units and standard errors are clustered by unit. Outcomes
          of one unit are dependent through a Gaussian copula with latent correlation rho() that
          leaves the marginal rates exact under either link (under probit this is the
          random-effects probit with rho = Var(c)/(Var(c) + 1)). design(cross-sections) draws
          independent observations, each after the treatment date with probability ppost()
          (default npost/(npre + npost)) and then in one of that block's periods with equal
          probability; n() counts all observations and se() chooses model-based or robust
          standard errors for the probit/logit analysis.
      
          More than two periods. The analysis includes an indicator for every period but the
          first and one treatment indicator, so it estimates one effect, not one per period. In
          a panel the dependence between two outcomes of a unit is the same however far apart
          the periods are (one shared unit component).  That is the favourable case for adding
          periods: the gain is larger than under dependence that fades with the distance between
          periods, which this version does not offer. (With rates of .20 and .30 in the control
          group, .40 and .60 in the treated group and rho(0.5), two periods on each side instead
          of one cut the variance of the linear panel estimate to 51%; if the latent correlation
          halved with every period of distance it would be 71%.) Treat the precision bought by
          extra periods as an upper bound.
      
          Comparing two subgroups. With subgroup(name, p(#)) both subgroups are treated in the
          treated group and the target is the difference between the two subgroups' effects on
          the treated, in probability points (the estimand of a triple difference that compares,
          say, the effect on women with the effect on men; Caron, 2025). baseline(), gap() and
          trend() may then hold two numbers, for the reference subgroup (name = 0) and for name
          = 1, and setatt takes the two effects. The probit/logit analysis is fully interacted
          with the subgroup and the estimate is the difference of the two subgroups' average
          partial effects of treatment (margins, dydx() subpop() over()); the linear analysis
          estimates the coefficient of the subgroup-by-treatment product. Trends are parallel
          within each subgroup, on the scale trends() names. The same eight rates imply
          different subgroup differences on the two scales (the design print shows both); a
          difference of zero in probability points is not a zero interaction coefficient when
          the subgroups have different rates; and with equal subgroups a difference as large as
          the effect itself needs about four times the units the effect needs. mde searches the
          difference, holding the reference subgroup's effect at its pinned value.
      
          The linear analysis. A linear probability model is heteroskedastic by construction, so
          its standard errors are always robust and se() is ignored: HC1 in cross-sections
          (regress, vce(robust)) and clustered by unit in panels (regress, vce(cluster), with
          its small-sample factor).  Ordinary least squares has no separation, so no simulated
          study is discarded for an all-zero or all-one cell; the sparse-cell warning still
          applies. In a balanced panel a time-constant covariate changes neither the estimate
          nor its clustered standard error beyond the degrees of freedom, so signal() buys the
          linear panel analysis no precision; in cross-sections it does.
      
          Interval coverage in small samples. Under the index analysis the effect on the treated
          is a nonlinear function of the estimated rates, and its delta-method interval (the one
          margins reports) covers slightly less than its nominal level when the group-by-period
          cells are small: for a 95% interval at a true effect of zero, 94.0% with 125
          observations per cell, 94.5% with 250, 94.7% with 500 and 94.9% with 1,000 under logit
          (exact values in the design without covariates). The simulated studies use this
          interval, so the power reported is the power of the analysis a study will run.
      
          A design from pre-treatment pilot data. powerape dgp name, didpilot estimates, from
          data in memory observed before the treatment date, the inputs such data can inform and
          builds the design: baseline() (the control group's rate; group() is the 0/1 indicator
          of the group that will be treated), gap(), ptreated() and, with id() and two or more
          periods per unit, rho() by the method of moments (the value at which the model's
          probability of the outcome in two periods of one unit equals the observed share of
          such pairs). All pre-treatment periods are pooled.  Pre-treatment data cannot show how
          the control group will change from before to after treatment, so trend() is required;
          with period() the control group's average change between pilot periods is reported as
          a guide.  Without id() the design is repeated cross-sections. Naming baseline(),
          gap(), ptreated(), rho() or design() overrides the estimate; covariates are not taken
          from the pilot, and subgroup designs are not supported. The estimates are used as if
          they were known: vary them with powerape robust, in particular for a small pilot (a
          note is printed below 500 rows).
      
          Limits of this version. One treated group, one treatment date, treatment assigned at
          the unit level, balanced panels, effects that are the same in every period after
          treatment, covariates that are constant over time and not interacted with period or
          treatment. Staggered adoption, period-specific effects, pre-trend tests and treatment
          assigned to whole clusters are not available (unit clustering would overstate power
          under cluster-level assignment).
      
      
      Engines
      
          engine(stata) (the default) writes every simulated study to a temporary frame and fits
          it with probit or logit (vce(robust) for se(robust), vce(cluster) on the panel route,
          asis for separation(keep)) or with cfprobit on the IV route. With effects(closed) the
          APE/AIE and its delta-method standard error are then computed in Mata from e(b) and
          e(V) by closed-form Jacobians; these equal margins, dydx() and ginteff to numerical
          precision (validation tier B). With effects(margins) the effects come from margins /
          ginteff themselves, which is several times slower.  On the IV route the APE is
          margins, dydx() after cfprobit, and the AIE is the difference of margins, dydx(focal)
          at(moderator=(0 1)):  the focal-by-moderator term is entered in factor form (1.d#c.m,
          the same fit as c.d#c.m) so that margins can differentiate the focal, and the control
          function is held at its estimated value, as in cfprobit's average structural function.
          (Setting the endogenous focal through at() instead would let the control function move
          and is not the AIE.) Both agree with the closed forms to 1e-7 (validation tier B).
          Inference follows Stata's conventions (observed information, Stata's small-sample
          factors).
      
          For difference-in-differences designs engine(stata) fits logit or probit with
          vce(cluster) in panels (model-based or vce(robust) in cross-sections) and computes the
          effect on the treated in closed form, or with effects(margins) by margins, dydx(w)
          subpop(if w == 1) (adding over(m) and taking the difference for a subgroup design);
          the two agree to 1e-16 on fixed data. The linear analysis is regress with vce(robust)
          or vce(cluster); effects() does not apply to it. engine(mata) uses Fisher scoring with
          the cluster sandwich, and ordinary least squares with the same HC1 and cluster factors
          as regress.
      
          engine(mata) is a self-contained Mata engine that reproduces the R package's
          conventions (Fisher scoring with expected information, HC0 and G/(G-1) cluster
          sandwiches, the stacked control-function covariance). It is about 10-30 times faster
          and is the right choice for long searches and sweeps; the two engines agree within
          Monte Carlo error (tiers A and D).
      
          Every simulation command (power, curve, n, mde, robust) prints a line fit: that names
          who fitted the simulated studies, for example fit: probit via engine(stata),
          effects(closed), fit: probit, vce(cluster) via engine(stata), effects(closed) on the
          panel route, fit: cfprobit via engine(stata), effects(margins) on the IV route, or
          fit: Mata engine (Fisher scoring); asis appears under separation(keep). The same text
          is stored in r(fit), with its parts in r(engine) and r(effects). powerape engine
          without options shows the session default.
      
      
      Separation and sparse cells
      
          A simulated study may contain an identifying cell (a level of a binary focal variable,
          a focal-by-moderator cell, or for difference-in-differences designs a
          group-by-before/after cell, within each subgroup if there is one) with no events or no
          non-events, where the maximum-likelihood effect does not exist. By default such
          replications count as failed (separation(fail)), as Stata's probit refuses the fit;
          separation(keep) retains them with their degenerate Wald intervals (R's glm
          behaviour). engine(mata) keeps the Fisher-scoring fit; engine(stata) fits with asis
          and keeps the separated coefficient, whose variance row Stata sets to zero (its
          standard error prints as missing), so the delta-method interval collapses onto the
          identified cells, which is the same limit: for a binary focal with an empty arm the
          estimate is minus the other arm's event rate, with that arm's binomial standard error.
          For difference-in-differences designs this holds under engine(mata) only:  under
          engine(stata) the asis fit is kept when the separated cell is the treated group's
          post-period cell (one coefficient without a variance), but when another
          group-by-before/after cell is separated several coefficients diverge together, Stata's
          fit does not converge and the study still counts as failed, so use engine(mata) with
          separation(keep) there. Terms omitted for collinearity still count as failures under
          either engine. The share with separation and the average smallest cell count are
          reported and warned about (more than 1% separated, or fewer than 10 events in the
          smallest cell).
      
      
      Stored results
      
          powerape power stores in r(): scalars r(power), r(mcse), r(n), r(nsim), r(alpha),
          r(conf), r(sesoi), r(target), r(separated), r(min_cell), r(n_failed); macros r(claim),
          r(estimand), r(model), r(se), r(separation), r(dgp), r(name), and r(fit), r(engine),
          r(effects) (who fitted the simulated studies, as on the fit: line of the output);
          matrix r(outcomes). curve adds r(results); n adds r(goal), r(confirmed),
          r(nsim_confirm), r(history); mde adds r(mde), r(is_margin), r(direction); robust
          stores r(scenarios), r(worst), r(nmax), r(nmax_power), r(nmax_scenario),
          r(nmax_candidates), r(fit), r(engine), r(effects); alpha stores r(alpha), r(power),
          r(beta), r(wcer), r(range_lo), r(range_hi), r(capped), r(alpha_uncapped), r(size) and
          r(grid); dgp, setape, setaie and describe store r(beta0), r(beta_focal), r(beta_mod),
          r(beta_int), r(target), r(p1), r(gamma); statement stores r(statement).
      
          Difference-in-differences designs add: after dgp, setatt and describe, macros
          r(design), r(trends), r(analysis), r(subgroup), scalars r(p_treated), r(p_post),
          r(rho), r(n_pre), r(n_post), r(reading_linear), r(reading_index) (the two readings of
          the rates; differences between the subgroups in a subgroup design), r(att_ref),
          r(att_sub), and matrices r(rates) (one row per subgroup: control before, control
          after, treated before, treated after without treatment, treated after) and r(didcoef)
          (the index coefficients a, b, l, th, de); didpilot adds r(pilot) (rows, units,
          periods, pairs, and the estimates). trueatt stores r(att) (for a subgroup design the
          difference, with r(att_ref) and r(att_sub)). power, n and mde add r(analysis),
          r(trends) and r(valid) (1 when the analysis assumes the scale the world has), and
          power adds r(est_mean) and r(est_mcse), the mean of the usable estimates and its Monte
          Carlo standard error; r(results) of curve gains a column est_mean. When robust varies
          trends, r(scenarios) codes it 1 = index, 2 = linear and gains the columns est_mean
          (power mode) and valid; scenarios with valid = 0 are shown but left out of the range,
          the worst case and n_max.
      
      
      Examples
      
              . powerape dgp d1, focal(treat, binary p(0.5)) covariates((age, normal mean(45)
                  sd(12))) baseline(0.30) signal(0.15)
              . powerape setape d1, target(0.05)
              . powerape power d1, n(2000) claim(minimum) sesoi(0.02) nsim(1000) seed(1)
              . powerape statement
              . powerape n d1, power(0.8) claim(detect) seed(2) engine(mata)
              . powerape mde d1, n(1500) claim(detect) seed(3) engine(mata)
              . powerape robust d1, n(1500) claim(detect) vary(baseline(0.2 0.4) signal(0 0.3))
                  seed(4) engine(mata)
      
              . powerape dgp d2, focal(treat, binary p(0.5)) moderator(female, binary p(0.55))
                  baseline(0.30)
              . powerape setaie d2, target(0.04) mainfocal(0.10) mainmoderator(0.05)
              . powerape power d2, n(4000) claim(detect) effects(margins)
      
              . probit y treat age female
              . powerape dgp f1, fromfit focal(treat)
              . powerape setape f1, target(0.10)
      
              . powerape dgp p1, panel focal(treat, binary p(0.5) icc(1)) covariates((size,
                  normal icc(0.6))) nperiods(4) rho(0.3) creshare(0.5) baseline(0.30)
                  signal(0.10)
              . powerape setape p1, target(0.08)
              . powerape power p1, n(150) claim(detect)
      
              . powerape dgp i1, iv focal(training, binary p(0.4)) covariates((size, normal))
                  instruments((subsidy, binary p(0.5))) endogeneity(0.4) ivstrength(0.2)
                  baseline(0.30) signal(0.10)
              . powerape setape i1, target(0.10)
              . powerape power i1, n(1500) claim(detect)
      
          Difference-in-differences: a logit panel, the linear analysis, a subgroup, a sweep
          over the scale of the trends
              . powerape dgp dd, did model(logit) baseline(0.20) gap(0.20) trend(0.10) rho(0.4)
              . powerape setatt dd, target(0.07)
              . powerape power dd, n(1500) claim(detect)
              . powerape n dd, power(0.8) claim(detect) engine(mata)
              . powerape robust dd, n(1500) claim(detect) vary(trends(index linear))
                  engine(mata)
              . powerape dgp dl, did baseline(0.20) gap(0.20) trend(0.10) rho(0.5)
                  trends(linear) analysis(linear) npre(2) npost(2)
              . powerape setatt dl, target(0.10)
              . powerape power dl, n(500) claim(detect)
              . powerape dgp ds, did model(logit) baseline(0.30 0.20) gap(0.10 0.15) trend(0.10
                  0.05) rho(0.5) subgroup(female, p(0.4))
              . powerape setatt ds, target(0.10 0.03)
              . powerape mde ds, n(4000) claim(detect) engine(mata)
              . powerape dgp dp, didpilot outcome(y) group(treated) id(firm) period(year)
                  trend(0.05) model(logit)
      
      
      Installation and requirements
      
          Install from the distribution directory (or a URL that serves it) with net install
          powerape, from(path); then run mata: mata mlib index once, so that the Mata library
          lpowerape.mlib is found, and powerape version to check that the ado-file and the
          library match. net get powerape, from(path) copies build.do (the .mata sources are
          installed with the package). In this project the alternative is adopath ++
          ".../powerape-stata/ado" followed by mata: mata mlib index.
      
          Requirements: Stata 16 or later (frames). The default engine(stata) on the IV route
          calls cfprobit (Stata 18 or later); engine(mata) covers every route on Stata 16 or
          later; effects(margins) for the AIE calls the community-contributed ginteff (Radean).
          The package was tested on StataNow 19.5 (update 14 Oct 2026) only. The shipped Mata
          library was compiled by StataNow 19.5; a library compiled by a newer Stata may not
          load in an older one, so on Stata 16-18 rebuild it by running build.do (it finds the
          installed sources on the adopath and replaces lpowerape.mlib in place) and then mata:
          mata mlib index.
      
      
      Validation
      
          The port is validated against the R package in four tiers (see
          powerape-stata/validation/RESULTS.md): exact finite-sample power by binomial
          enumeration; fixed-data parity of every estimator with R's internals and with
          probit/logit + margins, ginteff and cfprobit; calibration and inversion constants; and
          stochastic agreement of power, required n, MDE, robustness sweeps and justified alpha
          on matched designs. Version 0.1.1 adds the effects(margins) IV AIE and the Stata
          engine's separation(keep) to tiers A and B. Version 0.1.2 (also 2026-10-16) adds the
          fit: line and r(fit), r(engine), r(effects), a print-only change that leaves every
          validated number unchanged, and includes the fix by which a per-command engine() or
          effects() no longer alters the session default. Version 0.1.3 (2026-10-17) builds the
          unit index of a panel draw in one step instead of a loop, which makes panel
          calibration about as fast as in R (seconds instead of about a minute at the default
          nint()); it changes no number: the panel constants of tiers C and D are bit-identical
          to 0.1.2.
      
          Version 0.2.0 (2026-11-13) adds the difference-in-differences route, which has its own
          four tiers, against version 1.12.0 of the R package: exact power by enumeration of the
          four group-by-period cells under both engines and both analyses; fixed-data parity
          with R's internals and with logit/probit + margins, regress, didregress and
          xtdidregress; calibration constants; and stochastic agreement of power, rejection
          rates, required n, MDE, sweeps and justified alpha. The results of the routes that
          existed before are unchanged. Not ported from version 1.12.0 of the R package:  the
          three-way interaction design, and the linear probability analysis of the designs other
          than difference-in-differences.
